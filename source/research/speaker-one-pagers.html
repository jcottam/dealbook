Jensen Huang, Founder & CEO, NVIDIA

Summit: DealBook 2023 · Interviewer: Andrew Ross Sorkin · Length: ~29 min
Audio: 2023 / Audio only / jensen wong.wav · Video: 2023 / Live stream recordings / jensen wong.mp4

In one line: The man whose chips power the AI boom says AI still can't reason, gives a five-year timeline for AGI, and admits he wakes up every day worried, not proud.

Clip-worthy moments

  • 02:43 He delivered "the first AI supercomputer the world ever made" to OpenAI. It weighs 70 pounds, has 35,000 parts and sold for $250,000.
  • 07:03 Even now, AI can't do the step-by-step reasoning humans find easy: "We can't reason yet."
  • 07:58 AGI within five years, depending on how you define it.
  • 08:39 NVIDIA's own chips are designed with AI. None of them would be possible without it.
  • 13:55 U.S. chip supply-chain independence is "a decade to two decades away."
  • 17:57 America's lead over China in semiconductors: "call it a decade."
  • 20:00 He wakes up "worried and concerned," not proud and confident. On Andy Grove's "only the paranoid survive," he jokes that paranoia "needs therapy."
  • 21:46 He likes going home and telling his wife he saved the company that day.
  • 22:20 Why 50 direct reports? The people who report to the CEO "should require the least amount of pampering."
  • 23:57–25:21 If he'd known how hard it would be at 29, he wouldn't have started NVIDIA. The naive "how hard could it be?" attitude is "completely helpful, but also completely wrong."
  • 26:30 Deep learning "was not a chip problem." It means reinventing computing, and $1 trillion of data centers will be retooled.
